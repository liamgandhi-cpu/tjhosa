The chapterWhat HOSA is, and what ours is for

Not a club. A professional organization you happen to join in high school.

HOSA–Future Health Professionals is a global, student-led organization recognized by the U.S. Department of Education and the Department of Health and Human Services. Its mission is to empower members to become leaders in the global health community through education, collaboration, and experience.

Founded in 1976, HOSA is 100% health care — every part of its program points at one industry. That focus is what makes it useful. The people you meet through HOSA are the people you will apply alongside, train under, and eventually hire.

TJ's chapter takes that seriously. We are building this as a professional network with a competition season attached, not a competition team with a mailing list.

Who it's forNot just the pre-med crowd

This is not the pre-med club.

Health care is close to a fifth of the U.S. economy, and most of those jobs are not "doctor." HOSA has events across most of the field. Find the row closest to you.

  • Bio and lab work

    Lab technique, disease mechanisms, and biochemistry. Heavy overlap with a TJ senior research lab.Biomedical Laboratory Science · Pathophysiology · Biochemistry · Forensic Science

  • Public health

    Populations rather than patients: outbreaks, prevention, and who gets care.Epidemiology · Public Health · Community Awareness · Health Education

  • Biomedical engineering

    Build something that solves a clinical problem, then defend the design to a judge.Medical Innovation · Creative Problem Solving

  • Policy and pre-law

    Ethics, regulation, and argument, written and spoken.Medical Law and Ethics · Extemporaneous Writing — Health Policy · Biomedical Debate

  • Psych and neuro

    Mind, behavior, and development. These events tend to draw thinner fields, which works in your favor.Behavioral Health · Mental Health Promotion · Human Growth and Development

  • Clinical and emergency care

    Hands-on skills judged by people who do the job, plus certifications worth having anyway.Clinical Nursing · Sports Medicine · CPR / First Aid · Emergency Medical Technician

  • No idea yet

    Fine. Finding out that the thing you assumed you wanted isn't the thing you want is most of the point, and it's much cheaper to learn at sixteen.

What you getNetwork, mentorship, research, competition

Four things a club can't usually give you.

The competition season is the visible part. The network is the part that lasts past graduation.

A network with names in it

TJ produces an unusual concentration of people who go into medicine, research, and health policy — and then stay reachable. Our chapter is built to put current students in the same room as them.

  • Alumni admitted to BS/MD and other accelerated medical programs
  • Founders who have raised outside funding for health and biotech ventures
  • TJ alumni serving on our Board of Advisors
  • Clinicians, researchers, and policy staff across Virginia and Washington, DC

Mentorship, matched on purpose

We are building a mentorship program that pairs members with alumni and working professionals by interest — surgery, public health, computational biology, health law, device and diagnostics startups.

  • Structured pairings, not a one-time panel
  • Guidance on research placements and summer programs
  • Honest answers about what the training actually costs you

Research you can put your name on

Chapter-supported research studies, run with faculty and alumni advisors. Members who want a project get one; members who want to join a project can.

  • Study design, IRB and ethics basics, literature review
  • Direct path into Research Poster, Medical Innovation, and Biomedical Debate
  • Work that stands on its own outside the competition calendar

Competition, at every level

HOSA's competitive events program runs from the chapter through the Virginia State Leadership Conference to the International Leadership Conference. Six categories means there is a real event for a debater, a lab scientist, a writer, and a first responder.

  • Individual and team events
  • Knowledge exams, hands-on skills, and presentations
  • Scholarships and recognition at the state and international level

You get what you put into this.

Nobody will chase you for a form. The members who show up to prep sessions, take a research slot, and email the alumnus they were matched with are the ones for whom this chapter becomes the most useful thing on their transcript. That is the deal, stated plainly.

The rest of the yearService, speakers, workshops

Conferences are a few weekends. Everything else is the other thirty weeks.

Service

Blood drives, health screenings, volunteering, and the national HOSA Service Project, arranged as a group so you aren't cold-emailing hospitals alone at 11pm. Hours count toward the Recognition events.

Speakers

Physicians, nurses, PAs, EMTs, researchers, public health staff, and TJ alumni now in medical school, including the parts of the job nobody posts about.

Workshops

CPR and first aid, vitals, reading a chart, sterile technique. Things you do with your hands, not slides you sit through.

The region is the curriculum.

Northern Virginia and DC hold one of the densest concentrations of hospital systems, biotech firms, federal health agencies, and health policy work in the country. We are actively working to turn that proximity into access for members. These are conversations underway, and we will name partners here only once agreements are signed.

  • Regional hospital systems

    Health systems across Northern Virginia and the District, for shadowing, volunteer placements, judged skills practice, and speakers who can describe the job rather than the brochure.

  • The new Northern Virginia medical school

    A medical school is being planned for the region. A relationship built now — while it is standing up — is worth far more to TJ students than one negotiated a decade in.

  • Biotech, diagnostics, and health technology

    Companies along the Dulles corridor and the I-270 biotech cluster, including ventures founded by TJ alumni, for site visits, project sponsorship, and mentorship.

  • Health policy and public health

    Agencies, associations, and legislative offices in Richmond and DC. This chapter already has a legislative track record; we intend to keep using it.

JoinWho can join and how

If you go to TJ, you are eligible. That is the whole test.

  • Any TJ student, grades 9–12. No course prerequisite, no application, no tryout.
  • No prior health science background. Most members arrive without one.
  • You do not have to compete to belong — but the members who compete get the most out of the year.
  • Undecided is fine. Plenty of members join to find out whether medicine is actually for them. Finding out early is worth a lot.

What the first month looks like

An interest meeting and a chapter overview, then event selection with returning members who have already run the season once. You will pick a competitive event, get matched into a research or service project if you want one, and meet the first of the alumni we bring in.

QuestionsThe ones you're actually asking

Questions you're actually asking.

I'm not pre-med. Should I even be here?

Yes. There are events in biomedical engineering, health policy, epidemiology, forensics, behavioral health, and lab science. If your interest touches human health at any angle, including the data, the machines, the law, or the population, there's an event for it.

Do I need a health science class? TJ doesn't really have those.

No. Any TJ student can join. HOSA's secondary division covers students who are interested in a health career, whether or not their school runs a health science program.

Is this just another resume club?

It can be, if you treat it that way, and that's true of every club at TJ. The difference is that HOSA's standard is set outside the school. You either place at a conference or you don't. A judge who works in the field either signs off on your skill demo or doesn't. That's harder to manufacture than an officer title.

How much time does it take?

Chapter meetings are the baseline; the schedule is posted on ION. If you compete, add your own prep. A knowledge event like Medical Terminology is mostly studying on your own schedule. A skills event needs repeated practice with someone watching. A team event needs your team. Conferences fall on weekends.

Do I have to compete?

No. Service, speakers, workshops, and mentorship are open to every member. The Recognition events aren't a ranking either: you meet a published standard for things like service hours and get recognized for it.

What does it cost?

Dues are approximately $25 for the year, covering national, state, and chapter affiliation. Conference registration and travel are separate. If cost is a problem, email the chapter.

When is the deadline to join?

Virginia HOSA closes 2026–27 affiliation on December 18, 2026. You need to be on the chapter roster by then to compete this year. The affiliation form is in the chapter details above.

What if I join and hate it?

Then you spent a few hours ruling out a career path, which is a good trade at this age. Come to a couple of meetings before you decide.

How we got backThe short version

Back after a rule change.

TJ used to have a HOSA chapter. Virginia tied these organizations to career and technical education courses, and TJ didn't offer the qualifying health science course, so the chapter lost its charter. TJ students helped make the case for SB 707, which in 2024 let every public middle and high school in Virginia start one regardless of course offerings. That's why there are no prerequisites to join.

Va. Code § 22.1-227.1 · SB 707 (2024), ch. 776
