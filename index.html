We’re starting a chapter of HOSA–Future Health Professionals at TJ. It’s the national student organization for people headed into health, and it runs real competitions, clinical skills training, and service. 225,000+ members, chapters in every state. None of them were at TJ. Now one is.

What it is

A national organization, not a club somebody made up last spring.

HOSA is a CTSO, a Career and Technical Student Organization. That’s the same official category as DECA and FBLA, except every part of it is health. It has been running since 1976, it’s recognized by the U.S. Department of Education, and it holds competitions at the regional, state, and international level that thousands of students train for all year.

Its stated mission is “to empower HOSA–Future Health Professionals to become leaders in the global health community through education, collaboration, and experience.” What that cashes out to: you learn actual health content, you get scored on it by people who work in the field, and you spend the year around students who care about the same things you do.

225,000+ members · 51 chartered associations · founded 1976 · 60+ competitive events

Every U.S. state has one, plus D.C., Puerto Rico, American Samoa, Canada, Germany, and Italy.

Who it’s for

This is not the pre-med club.

Healthcare is close to a fifth of the U.S. economy and almost none of those jobs are “doctor.” HOSA has competitive events across most of the field, so pick whichever of these is closest to you:

  • Bio and lab work. Biotechnology, Genetics, Clinical Laboratory Science, Pathophysiology. Heavy overlap with a TJ senior research lab.
  • Public health. Epidemiology, Public Health, World Health & Disparities. Population level rather than patient level.
  • Biomedical engineering. Medical Innovation and Biomedical Equipment Technician. Build something, then defend the design to a judge.
  • Policy and pre-law. Medical Law & Ethics, Extemporaneous Writing in health policy, Biomedical Debate.
  • Psych and neuro. Behavioral Health, Mental Health Promotion, Human Growth & Development. Genuinely under-entered events, which works in your favor.
  • No idea yet. Fine. Finding out that the thing you assumed you wanted isn’t the thing you want is most of the point.

You don’t need a health science class for this. HOSA’s secondary division covers students enrolled in a health science program or students who are interested in and planning to pursue a health career. TJ doesn’t run a health science CTE pathway and it doesn’t need to.

Competition

Pick one or two events. Get seriously good at those.

There are 60+ events across six categories. Five of them are scored head to head. The sixth recognizes service and participation against a published standard instead of ranking you. Nobody does all of them.

  • Health Science

    Written exams. The closest thing to a subject test: you study a defined body of material and get ranked on it.

    Medical Terminology · Pathophysiology · Medical Math · Pharmacology · Nutrition · Medical Law & Ethics

  • Health Professions

    Hands-on clinical skill demos judged by people who do the job. You perform an actual procedure against an actual rubric.

    Clinical Nursing · Phlebotomy · Sports Medicine · Biotechnology · Veterinary Science · Physician Assistant

  • Emergency Preparedness

    Response under time pressure. Some of this is just competence you should have anyway.

    CPR/First Aid · Emergency Medical Technician · Epidemiology · Public Health · CERT Skills

  • Leadership

    Speaking, writing, and interviewing with a health angle. The events nobody signs up for and everybody needs.

    Prepared Speaking · Job Seeking Skills · Interviewing Skills · Extemporaneous Writing, Health Policy

  • Teamwork

    Two to six people, one score. Where a new chapter can catch up fastest, because chemistry matters more than seniority.

    HOSA Bowl · Biomedical Debate · Medical Innovation · Forensic Science · Creative Problem Solving · Research Poster

  • Recognition

    Not a ranking. You meet a published standard for service hours or the HOSA Service Project and you’re recognized for it.

    Service and volunteer recognition · HOSA Service Project · Chapter awards

The rest of the year

Conferences are three weekends. Everything else is the other thirty weeks.

  • Service

    Blood drives, clinic and senior-center volunteering, health screenings, and the national HOSA Service Project. Arranged as a group, so you aren’t cold-emailing hospitals alone at 11pm.

  • Speakers

    Physicians, nurses, PAs, EMTs, researchers, public health staff, and TJ alumni now in med school. Including the parts of the job nobody posts about.

  • Workshops

    CPR and first aid, vitals, suturing practice, reading a chart, PPE and sterile technique. Things you do with your hands, not slides you sit through.

Why bother

Why bother

You find out early. Some people get to their second year of med school before discovering they hate patient contact. Doing skill demos and talking to working clinicians at sixteen is a much cheaper way to learn that about yourself, and an equally good way to confirm it if you love it.

It’s specific. Admissions officers have read a great deal of “I shadowed a doctor and it inspired me.” Placing at the Virginia State Leadership Conference in Pathophysiology, or running a CPR workshop for sixty people, is a different kind of sentence because it has an outcome attached. HOSA runs its own scholarship program on top of that.

You get the people. Everyone here is interested in some corner of the same enormous field. That means study partners in your event, teammates who actually show up, upperclassmen who’ve already done the applications, and a few hundred students from other Virginia schools once you get to states.

Questions

Questions you’re actually asking

Is this just another resume club?

It can be, if you treat it that way, and that’s true of every club at TJ. The difference is that HOSA has a standard set outside the school. You either place at regionals or you don’t. A judge who works in the field either signs off on your skill demo or doesn’t. That is harder to manufacture than an officer title.

If you want to show up, pay dues, and never compete, we’ll take you and the speakers and workshops are worth it. But the good part is on the other side of picking an event.

I’m not pre-med. Should I even be here?

Yes. There are events in biomedical engineering, health policy, epidemiology, forensics, behavioral health, health informatics, and biotechnology. If your interest touches human health at any angle, including the data, the machines, the law, or the population, there’s an event for it.

Do I need to be taking a health science class? TJ doesn’t really have those.

No. HOSA’s secondary division covers students in a health science program or students who are interested in and planning to pursue a career in the health professions. Plenty of chapters run out of schools with no health science CTE pathway. That’s our situation and it’s a normal one.

How much time is this actually going to take?

Baseline is [TIME_COMMITMENT] for meetings. That’s the floor, and plenty of members stay there.

If you compete, add your own prep. A knowledge event like Medical Terminology is mostly flashcards on your own schedule. A skills event needs repeated practice with someone watching. A team event needs your team. Regionals is a day and states is about three, both on weekends, so they don’t collide with a problem set due Tuesday.

Do I have to compete?

No. Service, speakers, and workshops run all year and are open to every member. The Recognition category isn’t a ranking either: you meet a published standard for things like service hours and get recognized for it without ever going head to head.

We’re brand new. Are we going to get destroyed?

In some events, at first, probably. Chapters that have run for a decade have a bench and a filing cabinet of old prep material. We have neither yet.

So pick strategically in year one. Knowledge events reward individual study, so a motivated first-year competitor can place in one. Newer and less-entered events like behavioral health, health policy writing, and medical innovation have thinner fields than Medical Terminology. And a first-year chapter that sends three people to states is a real result, not a consolation prize.

What if I join and hate it?

Then you spent approximately $25 and a few hours ruling out a career path, which is a good trade at this age. Come to the first couple of meetings before dues are due and decide then. The interest form doesn’t commit you to anything.
